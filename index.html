Research

I am an economist working to understand how markets behave and what that means for the broader economy, with particular attention to rare but economically consequential events. Financial crises are, statistically, tail events: infrequent but consequential, so my research models their timing and intensity rather than the calm middle of the distribution, and traces how extreme risk in one market spills over into others and into the real economy.

Methodologically, I work at the intersection of extreme value theory, point processes, and score-driven (observation-driven) time-series models, building tools to forecast Value at Risk, Expected Shortfall, and systemic tail risk. Empirically, I apply them to banking, commodities, electricity, and currency markets, often with high-frequency and option-implied information.

Extreme value theory

Peaks-over-threshold and self-exciting models for the dynamics of rare market events.

Quantitative risk management

Forecasting VaR, Expected Shortfall and expectiles; evaluating and backtesting tail risk.

Systemic & spillover risk

How extreme risk propagates across banks, commodities and energy markets.
